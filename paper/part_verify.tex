\section{Software and verification}
\label{sec:verify}

\subsection{The package}

The ladder, the simulations, the rule and the classifiers form a Python package,
\code{rvvet} (version \nmrvvetVersion), with a command-line interface that writes the
features of one star as JSON together with a provenance block (package version, a
hash of the source files, the input file and the arguments). It has 40 unit tests.
Thirteen compare the code with an expected value obtained another way: the projected
periodogram against dense least squares, its invariance to era offsets, the nightly
means, a circular Keplerian against a sinusoid, the analytic $(G, G')$ covariance
against finite differences, the single-frequency limit of the FAP, the ordering of the
likelihood-ratio and fixed-jitter statistics, the mean of the homogeneity statistic against
its $\chi^2$ expectation, and hand-worked cases for the night boundary, the harmonic
distances, the rule and the labels. The other 27 check behaviour on constructed
inputs: recovery of injected periods, amplitudes and jitters, the Baluev approximation not
exceeding its nominal rate for Gaussian noise of known variance, the alias rung staying
inside the search band, the window around a claimed period, the detrending and clipping of
indicators, the exclusion of seasonal aliases from the rotation proxy, a transient versus
a stationary signal, removal of an $e = 0.6$ orbit, deterministic seeding, folds that
never split a star, and the command line. A simulated case takes about
\nmSecPerCase\,s on one core. The results come from Python 3.13 with \code{numpy} 2.4,
\code{scipy} 1.17, \code{pandas} 3.0, \code{scikit-learn} 1.8 and \code{matplotlib} 3.10, pinned
in the release; in that environment the released code regenerates the benchmark features,
\nmConsistN\ randomly chosen simulated cases and every number, table and figure exactly. Newer versions differ slightly: with
\code{scikit-learn} \nmVerOther, which assigns stars to cross-validation folds differently and
builds different trees, the classifier AUCs on the simulations move by at most \nmVerAucMax\ and
the benchmark AUC without indicator features changes from \nmVerBNoIndPaper\ to
\nmVerBNoIndOther. Every computed result in the text of this paper is a macro written by the
analysis scripts from their outputs (Sect.~\ref{sec:timesplit} by its own script), and so is
every table body except Table~\ref{tab:features}; the numbers typed by hand are design constants, literature values
and counts of items such as tests, listed in the released provenance file.

\subsection{Defects found in an AI-assisted analysis}

The HD\,297396\,b analysis that motivated this work was carried out by one author
with an AI assistant (Claude, Anthropic) that wrote most of the analysis code and
draft text, while the author made the analysis decisions. Its revisions were checked
in up to four ways: \emph{self-review}, in which the changed numbers were checked
against their scripts, source papers and catalogues; an \emph{independent reviewer},
a separate instance of the model given the manuscript, the source papers and the
scripts but not the conversation that produced them, and asked to act as a referee;
an \emph{external report}, a review of version 13 (the ``Referee-Lens'' report)
whose requests version 14 implemented; and, for version 15, an automated \emph{reference check} of every entry
against the publisher's record. Each version has a change log.

\emph{Method.} We extracted the defects from the records written at the time: the
change logs of versions 13, 14 and 15, the referee report on version 15 and the
reference check. A codebook fixed before any coding (released with the code) defines
the unit: a statement, number, figure element, reference or analysis step that a
record says was changed because it was wrong, unsupported, stale, inconsistent or
ambiguous, counted once per root cause. New analyses, restructuring and cosmetic
fixes are not defects. An instance of the model that had not seen any earlier
tabulation extracted \nmDefN\ defects, listing the \nmDefExcluded\ record items it
considered and excluded, with reasons; who found each defect (the \emph{route}) and
whether it was in a released version or caught in a draft (the \emph{stage}) are read
from the records. Two further instances, blind to each other, coded each defect's
category and origin; they are instances of the same model, so their agreement measures the
consistency of the codebook's application rather than independent human judgement. They agreed on \nmDefAgreeCat\% of the categories (Cohen's
$\kappa = \nmDefKappaCat$) and \nmDefAgreeOri\% of the origins
($\kappa = \nmDefKappaOri$); the \nmDefDisagree\ defects with any disagreement were
settled by a third reading, with the reason recorded. One coder read two rows of the
list of excluded items before coding; neither row is a coded defect.

\emph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:defects} gives the \nmDefN\ defects by category and
route; \nmDefReleased\ were in released versions and \nmDefDraft\ were caught in
drafts. The largest category, \nmDefOverPct\% of the total, is overclaims
(\nmDefCatOverclaim), statements stronger than the evidence or caveats that were missing: a simulation presented as testing a
scenario its kernel could not produce, a weak out-of-sample test described as
agreement, companions said to be excluded out to 100\,au when the contrast curve
stopped near 55\,au. Revising the text was itself a source: \nmDefOriRev\ defects
were introduced by revision, \nmDefRevOver\ of them overclaims, mostly caveats that the
rewrite of version 14 dropped. Of the \nmDefNumN\ numerical errors (transcription and
arithmetic), \nmDefNumLit\ came from the literature, such as parameters of comparison
planets that did not match their source papers, \nmDefNumAna\ from calculations in the
analysis, \nmDefNumRev\ from revision, and \nmDefNumCode\ were numbers the project's own
code had produced correctly and that were carried into the text wrongly, among them the
candidate's own semi-amplitude pasted into the column of a comparison planet. Over all
categories, only \nmDefOriCode\ defects had that origin; errors of method, such as an
activity index on one calibration scale fed into a relation built on another, came from
the analysis itself.

The independent reviewer found \nmDefRouteRev\ of the \nmDefN, including
\nmDefOverRev\ of the \nmDefCatOverclaim\ overclaims; self-review found \nmDefRouteSelf,
the external report \nmDefRouteExt\ and the reference check \nmDefRouteRef; for
\nmDefRouteUnatt\ the record does not say. These shares depend on the order of the
checks, since the reviewer saw what self-review had not caught, and on which record exists
for each version (for version 15 the main record is the referee report); the records were
written by the same assistant whose work they describe. The log counts the defects
that were found, not those that remain, for one project and one model.

The same checks were applied to this paper. The first draft reported 30 defects, not
\nmDefN: it had condensed the records by hand, its route column contradicted its own
notes, and it concluded that most defects arose where numbers passed from code into
text. That draft also contained four errors in the pipeline (the night boundary, the
alias rung, the fixed jitter and the degrees of freedom of the block test;
Sect.~\ref{sec:changes}). The independent review of the draft found all of them, and two further rounds of review found more (Sect.~\ref{sec:changes}). The night-boundary error also
affected two archive-wide numbers in the analysis of HD\,297396\,b, though no result about
the star; it was corrected there after release. The log describes one project rather than
testing a hypothesis, but it is consistent with two practices: a reviewer that sees the
sources but not the history, which found most of what was found, including most
overclaims; and checks aimed at what the text claims, not only at whether its numbers
match the code, since generating every number from the analysis outputs, as this paper
now does, addresses only a small share of the defects.
